# What's new in C# and .NET

.NET ships every **November**. Even-numbered versions are **LTS** (3 years of support),
odd-numbered ones are STS (2 years). Each .NET release comes with a new C# version.

| C# | .NET | Year | Highlights | Learn it in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8  | Core 3 | 2019 | nullable reference types, switch expressions, async streams, ranges `^1..` | 01, 02, 12 |
| 9  | 5  | 2020 | **records**, init-only properties, top-level statements, relational patterns | 05, 11 |
| 10 | 6 LTS | 2021 | file-scoped namespaces, global usings, record structs | 15 |
| 11 | 7  | 2022 | raw string literals `"""`, **list patterns**, `required` members, generic math | 01, 05, 08, 11 |
| 12 | 8 LTS | 2023 | **primary constructors**, **collection expressions** `[1, 2]`, spread `..` | 04, 05 |
| 13 | 9  | 2024 | `params` collections, the `Lock` type, LINQ `CountBy` / `Index` | 10, 12 |
| 14 | 10 LTS | 2025 | **extension members** (properties, static), the `field` keyword, `?.=` assignment, **file-based apps** (`dotnet run file.cs`) | 05, 14 |

`14_modern_csharp/a_modern_features.cs` shows one example from each version.

C# 15 / .NET 11 arrive in November 2026 (in preview now). Follow along at
https://learn.microsoft.com/dotnet/csharp/whats-new/
